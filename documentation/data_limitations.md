# Data Limitations and Interpretation

## Synthetic-data disclosure

This is an independent portfolio project built with synthetic learning data. It is not data from an actual oil and gas company, field, facility, contractor or workforce. Findings must not be represented as actual industry or operator performance.

## Sampling and completeness

The production and cost records are generated/sampled datasets and may not represent complete operational logs or audited financial ledgers. A pattern in the available records may not generalise beyond this dataset or period.

## Safety-rate denominators

A reliable Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR) or Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR) requires an appropriate workforce-hours exposure denominator. Workforce-hours are not available in the described source datasets. Do not calculate or publish TRIR/LTIFR from incident counts alone.

Incident counts and the percentage of recorded incidents classified as lost-time injuries can be reported if the underlying incident fields support them, but these are not exposure-adjusted safety rates.

## Production and downtime interpretation

A relationship between downtime and production volume or variance is an association unless the analysis establishes a defensible causal design. Production can also be influenced by reservoir conditions, planned maintenance, well interventions, facility constraints, operating decisions and data coverage.

Do not label all downtime as unplanned, or attribute all production shortfall to equipment failures, unless the source fields and definitions support that classification.

## Financial interpretation

Cost per barrel, budget variance and maintenance share must use aligned time periods, appropriate production denominators, consistent currencies and non-duplicated cost records. Do not treat a high absolute cost as inefficiency without considering asset scale, location, production contribution and operating context.

## Statistical interpretation

Report the method used, sample size, variables, assumptions and relevant test statistics. A statistically significant association does not automatically imply a material operational effect or causation. Where assumptions are not met, select a suitable alternative or describe the result as exploratory.

## Reporting rules

1. State the reporting period and unit for each KPI.
2. Distinguish missing values from zero values.
3. Identify any exclusions or filters.
4. Avoid unsupported estimates and fabricated denominators.
5. Document known data-quality issues and their treatment.
6. Label recommendations as follow-up actions where the analysis is indicative rather than conclusive.
